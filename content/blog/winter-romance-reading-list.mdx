There is a specific kind of reading that belongs to winter. Not the light, summery reads that carry you through July — no. Winter reading should be heavy, atmospheric, and laced with the kind of longing that feels appropriate when the world outside is grey and frozen.

These are the books I keep returning to when December arrives and I want to feel *things*.

## The Reread Shelf

**A Winter's Promise** by Christelle Dabos — This is a book I return to every single winter without fail. The world-building is unlike anything else I have read, and the central relationship develops with a patience that would be frustrating if it weren't so beautifully earned.

**Spinning Silver** by Naomi Novik — Cold, sharp, and smarter than any fairy tale reworking has a right to be. The Staryk king is one of the most compelling love interests in the genre.

**The Bear and the Nightingale** by Katherine Arden — Set in a Russian winter that feels mythological, with magic woven into the frost itself. I have read this three times.

## New to My Shelf This Year

**The Foxglove King** by Hannah Whitten — Death magic, a crumbling court, and two men who both have terrible ideas. The winter I read this, I did very little else.

**In a Holidaze** by Christina Lauren — Yes, it's a holiday romance. No, I am not above it. The atmosphere is perfect for December.

## What I'm Writing Into Winter

My current work-in-progress — which I am not ready to say much about yet — is a winter story. It is the coldest thing I have written, in every sense. There is snow, obviously. There is a house that shouldn't be there. There is a man who has been waiting long enough to be dangerous.

That is all I can tell you.
