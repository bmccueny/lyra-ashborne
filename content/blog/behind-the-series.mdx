I started writing *The Shattered Realm* on a Tuesday night in October, which I remember only because of the moon. It was enormous and orange — the kind of moon that feels like a rumour. I had been walking for an hour because I couldn't sleep, and somewhere between the park and my front door, a character walked into my head and wouldn't leave.

She was angry. She was cold. She was carrying something heavy that she hadn't named yet.

She was Serafine.

## The Character Who Started Everything

Serafine Ash came before the plot, before the world, before even the title. She arrived fully formed in one specific emotional state: the exhaustion of being the only person in a room who can see the disaster coming. She had been right, too many times, about too many things that no one wanted to hear. And she had run out of patience.

I wrote six pages that night. They are not in the book. They are embarrassing in the way that all first drafts are embarrassing — too earnest, too on-the-nose. But they taught me who she was.

## The Villain Who Became a Love Interest

Dorian was supposed to be the antagonist. Fully. Irredeemably.

He refused.

This is, I'm told, a common experience among fantasy romance writers, and I always believed it in the abstract. I did not expect it to happen to me so completely, so early. By chapter four, Dorian was explaining himself in ways that made a horrible amount of sense. By chapter eight, I was watching him do something quietly kind when he thought no one was looking.

I argued with him for two weeks. He won.

## Building the World

The Shattered Realm is a world that has already broken once. The fracture lines are still visible — in the politics, in the architecture, in the way certain characters flinch at certain words. I wanted a world that carried its history in its bones, because that felt true: places remember things, the same way people do.

The magic system grew out of the question: *what do you sacrifice to hold something together?* The answer shaped everything else.
